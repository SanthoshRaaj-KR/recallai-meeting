# Mission and Values

Nimbus Robotics builds collaborative robot arms that make precision automation affordable for small and mid-sized manufacturers. Our mission is to put a reliable robotic teammate within reach of every workshop floor. We are guided by four values: safety first, transparency by default, customer obsession, and disciplined craftsmanship. These values are not slogans; they are the tie-breakers we use when a decision is genuinely hard. New initiatives are evaluated against them during planning.

# Code of Conduct

Every employee is expected to treat colleagues, customers, and partners with respect and professionalism. Harassment, discrimination, and retaliation of any kind are strictly prohibited and are grounds for termination. We assume good intent, give direct and kind feedback, and disagree without being disagreeable. Concerns about conduct can be raised with any manager or with People Ops, and all reports are handled confidentially. We hold leaders to a higher standard, not a lower one.

# Working Hours and Flexibility

Core collaboration hours are 10 AM to 4 PM in each employee's local time zone, when meetings and synchronous work are scheduled. Outside of core hours, employees are trusted to set their own start and end times to fit their lives. We measure output and impact, not hours logged or seat time. Teams that span time zones agree on a shared overlap window and document it. Sustainable pace is a long-term performance strategy, not a perk.

# Meeting Etiquette

Every recurring meeting must have a written agenda shared at least 24 hours in advance, and meetings without an agenda may be respectfully declined. Each meeting has an owner responsible for outcomes, notes, and action items with owners. We default to the smallest group that can make the decision, and we end early when the work is done. Decisions and their rationale are written down so people who were not in the room can follow along. Recurring meetings are reviewed quarterly and cancelled if they no longer earn their place.

# Communication Norms

Urgent, time-sensitive issues go through the on-call pager or a phone call, never a buried chat message. Non-urgent discussion happens in shared team channels rather than private direct messages, so context stays visible and searchable. We write clearly, lead with the conclusion, and provide the supporting detail underneath. Long-form proposals are documented and circulated for asynchronous comment before any meeting. We assume our messages may be read by people outside the immediate thread and write accordingly.

# Decision Making

We use a lightweight decision framework that names a single accountable decision-maker for each significant choice. The decision-maker gathers input widely but is not required to reach consensus. Once a decision is made, we disagree-and-commit and move forward as a team. Significant decisions are recorded with the context, the options considered, and the reasoning. Reversible decisions are made quickly; one-way-door decisions get more deliberation.

# Travel and Reimbursement

Business travel should be booked through the approved travel platform at a reasonable cost. Employees are reimbursed for necessary business expenses with itemized receipts submitted within thirty days. Economy fares are the default for flights under six hours. Personal travel combined with business trips must clearly separate the personal portion, which is not reimbursable. When in doubt, employees are asked to spend company money as if it were their own.

# Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion

We are committed to building a team that reflects the diversity of the customers and communities we serve. Hiring panels are structured to reduce bias, and interview questions are standardized for each role. Pay is reviewed regularly to identify and correct unexplained gaps. Every employee has access to inclusion training and to employee resource groups. Inclusive behavior is part of how performance is evaluated for managers.

# Learning and Development

Each employee has an annual learning budget for courses, conferences, and books that grow job-relevant skills. Managers and reports set development goals during quarterly check-ins and revisit them throughout the year. Internal lunch-and-learns and brown-bag sessions are encouraged and supported. Time spent learning is considered part of the job, not an extracurricular activity. We promote from within whenever a qualified internal candidate is available.
